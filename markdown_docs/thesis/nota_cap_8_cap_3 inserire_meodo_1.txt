Let $s_i$ denote the policy score assigned to patient $i$. 
At each enrollment round, patients are ranked according to 
\[
s_i,
\]
and the probability of selecting a patient is controlled by a temperature parameter 
For $\tau>0$. 
For $\tau>0$, the selection mechanism can be expressed as
\[
P(i \mid \mathcal{P}) =
\frac{\exp(s_i/\tau)}
{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}}\exp(s_j/\tau)}.
\]

where $\mathcal{P}$ denotes the set of patients currently available for enrollment. 
A higher temperature produces a flatter distribution, increasing exploration by 
allowing patients with lower scores to be selected, whereas a lower temperature 
concentrates the probability mass on patients with the highest scores. The temperature 
is progressively reduced during enrollment according to
\[
\tau(n) = \tau_0
\exp\!\left(-\lambda\frac{n}{N}\right).
\]
where $\tau_0$ is the initial temperature, $\lambda$ controls the rate of
decay, $n$ is the number of patients enrolled, and $N$ is the total cohort size. 
Consequently, early enrollment gives greater weight to exploration, 
while later rounds increasingly exploit the learned ranking. 
In the limiting case $\tau\rightarrow0$, the procedure converges to 
deterministic top-$k$ selection, so that the patients with the highest 
policy scores are preferentially enrolled.
