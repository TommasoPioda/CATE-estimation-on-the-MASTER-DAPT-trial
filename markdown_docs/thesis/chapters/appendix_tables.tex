\section{The trade-off plane: individual and pairwise ischemic endpoints}
\label{sec:appendix-tradeoff-plane}

Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff-plane} in Chapter~\ref{chap:06}
combines MI, stroke and cardiovascular death into a single weighted ischemic
axis ($0.4$, $0.4$ and $0.2$ respectively). The two figures below unpack that
combination from both sides: bleeding against each ischemic endpoint kept
separate, and the three ischemic endpoints plotted directly against one
another. Both use the same tuned causal forest as the main-text figure.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{CATE_tradeoff_individual_endpoints.png}
\caption{Bleeding benefit of abbreviating against each of the three ischemic
endpoints kept separate (MI, stroke, cardiovascular death), one column per
endpoint. Top row, patient density; bottom row, the same points tinted by
the net benefit of abbreviating on that pair of axes. The tension is
endpoint-specific: MI carries most of the ischemic cost of abbreviating,
while stroke and cardiovascular death are closer to symmetric around zero.}
\label{fig:tradeoff-individual-endpoints}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{CATE_ischaemic_pairwise.png}
\caption{The three ischemic endpoints' CATE plotted against one another,
one point per patient, coloured by patient density. None of the three pairs
shows a tight positive relationship, i.e.\ a patient predicted to benefit
from abbreviating on one ischemic endpoint is not reliably predicted to
benefit on another.}
\label{fig:ischaemic-pairwise}
\end{figure}

\section{Mechanism 1: single-endpoint ischemic-weight robustness check}

Table~\ref{tab:ch8-ischaemic-rate-comparison} in Chapter~\ref{ch:guided-enrollment-applied}
reports the enrolled-vs-left-out ischemic separation under the standard composite
weighting, $\text{ISCH\_WEIGHTS} = \{\text{death}: 0.2, \text{mi}: 0.4, \text{stroke}: 0.4\}$.
Table~\ref{tab:appendix-mech1-mi-only} reports a separate Mechanism~1 run that stopped at
$n=4200$, rather than the current main configuration's $n=3500$, with the composite
collapsed onto myocardial infarction alone
($\text{ISCH\_WEIGHTS} = \{\text{death}: 0, \text{mi}: 1, \text{stroke}: 0\}$), to check
whether the composite's separation holds when the weighting is restricted to a single
endpoint. Conflict denotes $\text{bleeding}-\text{ischemic}$ and Net-benefit/win-win
denotes $\text{bleeding}+\text{ischemic}$.

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \small
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{6pt}
    \begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
        \toprule
        Policy & Selected (\%) & Left-out (\%) & Diff. (pp) & SEM (pp) & $p$ \\
        \midrule
        Conflict           & 4.57 & 3.65 & $+0.92$ & 0.14 & $<0.001$ \\
        Net-benefit        & 4.24 & 6.38 & $-2.14$ & 0.19 & $<0.001$ \\
        Angle net-benefit  & 4.23 & 6.51 & $-2.29$ & 0.19 & $<0.001$ \\
        Angle conflict     & 4.58 & 3.57 & $+1.01$ & 0.15 & $<0.001$ \\
        Random             & 4.48 & 4.39 & $+0.09$ & 0.12 & 0.458 \\
        $-$isch            & 4.65 & 2.99 & $+1.66$ & 0.14 & $<0.001$ \\
        $+$isch            & 4.19 & 6.82 & $-2.63$ & 0.21 & $<0.001$ \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Comparison of MI event rates between enrolled and left-out patients at
    $n=4200$, rather than the $n=3500$ checkpoint used in
    Table~\ref{tab:ch8-ischaemic-rate-comparison}, with the ischemic composite restricted
    to myocardial infarction
    ($\text{ISCH\_WEIGHTS} = \{\text{death}: 0, \text{mi}: 1, \text{stroke}: 0\}$).
    Results are averaged over 100 runs, with 379 patients remaining outside the enrolled
    cohort. A negative difference indicates a lower MI event rate among enrolled
    patients.}
    \label{tab:appendix-mech1-mi-only}
\end{table}
