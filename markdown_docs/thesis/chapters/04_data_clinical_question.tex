\section{Dual Antiplatelet Therapy after Percutaneous Coronary Intervention}

Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) is a widely used procedure for
treating coronary artery disease. It involves the mechanical widening of
a narrowed coronary artery, typically followed by the deployment of a
drug-eluting stent (DES) to maintain vessel patency. After stent
implantation, patients are at risk of stent thrombosis — a potentially
life-threatening acute occlusion — that is mitigated by Dual Antiplatelet
Therapy (DAPT), combining aspirin with a P2Y12 inhibitor (such as clopidogrel,
ticagrelor, or prasugrel).

The optimal duration of DAPT after PCI is one of the most debated topics in
interventional cardiology. Longer DAPT reduces ischemic risk (particularly
stent thrombosis and myocardial infarction) but simultaneously increases the
risk of bleeding complications. Major bleeding events are not merely
nuisance side effects: they are independently associated with increased
mortality, rehospitalization, and quality-of-life impairment. The trade-off
between ischemic protection and bleeding harm is therefore central to the
clinical decision.


\section{The Trial, and the Question That Survives Its Conclusion}

MASTER DAPT \citep{valgimigli2021masterdapt} randomized 4,579 high bleeding risk patients, after coronary
stent implantation, between abbreviated and prolonged dual antiplatelet
therapy. Randomization took place one month after the index procedure, in
patients who were free of events up to that point: \emph{that visit is the
temporal landmark of the whole analysis}, and every covariate used in this
thesis is measured at or before it.

The trial concluded in favour of the abbreviated strategy — noninferior on
ischemic events, superior on bleeding \citep{valgimigli2021masterdapt}. That is an average result, and it is
the concrete instance of the problem posed in Chapter 1: a favourable ATE is
perfectly compatible with subgroups that gain nothing, or that are actively
harmed, and the trial on its own does not say which of the two this cohort
contains. The question that survives the trial's conclusion is therefore not
\emph{whether} abbreviating is better on average — that is settled — but
\emph{for whom} it is better, and by how much.

\section{The Cohort}

The thesis dataset contains the 4,579 randomized patients, split almost
exactly in half by the randomization (2,295 abbreviated, 2,284 prolonged),
described by 63 baseline covariates plus the treatment indicator. The
covariates were selected from the full trial case report form through a
combination of predictive screening and consultation with the study domain
experts, and cover demographic, clinical, laboratory and procedural
information: 15 are continuous measurements (age, laboratory values such as
creatinine and haemoglobin, procedural quantities such as contrast
volume) and the remaining 48 are binary or categorical indicators of
comorbidities, concomitant medications and procedural characteristics.

Missing values are deliberately \emph{not} imputed in the shared dataset.
Eight covariates carry missing entries — most notably pre-procedural glycemia,
platelet count and creatinine — and every imputation is deferred to the
individual modelling pipelines, where it is fitted inside the
cross-validation folds to avoid leakage of information across folds.
 Imputing once, up front, on the full dataset would
leak information across folds and inflate every performance estimate
downstream.

\paragraph{Sign convention.}
The coding of the treatment is fixed for the entire thesis: $T = 1$ denotes
\emph{prolonged} therapy and $T = 0$ denotes \emph{abbreviated} therapy with $X$ 
being the vector of baseline covariates and $Y$ being the outcome. The
outcomes coded as adverse events, the conditional average treatment effect
\[
  \tau(x) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[\,Y(1) - Y(0) \mid X = x\,\right]
\]
is therefore the excess event risk caused by prolonging, which is exactly the
\emph{benefit of abbreviating}. A positive $\tau(x)$ means that patients with
profile $x$ are in a superior position with respect to the abbreviated strategy; a negative $\tau(x)$
means they are more favorable to the prolonged one; 
a value near zero means the
choice is immaterial for that patient type. This convention is used without
exception in every chapter that follows, so that the sign of an estimate can
always be read directly as \emph{abbreviate} or \emph{do not abbreviate}.

\paragraph{Endpoints.}
Of the nine adjudicated binary endpoints available at 335 days, five are
considered throughout the thesis; they are reported in Table~\ref{tab:endpoints}.
Time-to-event information is deliberately not used. Instead, outcomes are represented by their binary 
status at 335 days, consistent with the outcome definition used in the trial's primary analysis \citep{valgimigli2021masterdapt}.
This choice sacrifices information on the timing of events in favour of a simpler classification 
framework, which is adopted throughout the thesis to facilitate the application of 
the causal modelling methods introduced in the following chapters.

The two bleeding endpoints are by far the most frequent, at 11.1\% and 7.8\%
respectively; the BARC 2/3/5 definition is the more restrictive of the two,
which is why it records fewer events than overall bleeding — the difference
consists of the milder BARC 1 episodes, which do not require medical
attention. The three ischemic endpoints are considerably less frequent, with myocardial
infarction at 2.4\%, cardiovascular death at 1.8\% and stroke at 0.8\%.

Table~\ref{tab:endpoints} also makes explicit how imbalanced these endpoints
are: event counts span more than an order of magnitude, from 506 bleeding
events down to 35 strokes, and three of the five endpoints — myocardial
infarction, cardiovascular death and stroke — fall below 110 events overall.
This imbalance reflects the composition of the MASTER DAPT population, which consisted exclusively of patients at 
high bleeding risk, resulting in substantially more observations for bleeding outcomes than for ischemic outcomes.

\paragraph{Two Important Characteristics of the Study Population.}
First, \emph{every} patient in this cohort is at high bleeding risk, because
that was an eligibility criterion \citep{valgimigli2021appendix} and not an accident of the randomization.
The cohort is therefore a deliberately restricted slice of the PCI population,
not a sample of it: the spread of bleeding risk within it is compressed by
construction The restricted range of 
bleeding risk in this cohort limits the model's ability to distinguish between patients.

Second, ischemic events are an order of magnitude rarer than bleeding events,
with cardiovascular death and stroke both below one hundred observations. Any
heterogeneity in the ischemic arm  has to be detected from
that handful of events, which caps in advance how much signal can be extracted from the data.

\paragraph{Admissibility of the \texttt{fup\_} covariates.}
Three of the 63 covariates carry the prefix \texttt{fup\_} —
\texttt{fup\_other}, \texttt{fup\_nitrates} and \texttt{fup\_insulin} — which
suggests information recorded after treatment assignment, and therefore
leakage. The prefix refers to the section of the trial's case report form in
which the variables appear, not to the time at which they are measured: the
three values are recorded at the one-month visit, which is the landmark defined
above, and they are consequently baseline covariates in the sense required
here, measured before the intervention whose effect is estimated. They are
therefore retained. The same criterion applies to all 63 covariates:
admissibility is determined by the recording date relative to the landmark, and
not by the name of the variable

\section{A First Look at the Data}

Before any modelling, two exploratory questions are worth settling: whether
the cohort visibly separates into subgroups, and whether obvious pairwise
structure among the covariates exists.

\paragraph{Structure and clustering.}
Principal component analysis was applied to the 15 continuous covariates, after a
$\log(1+x)$ transform of the eight right-skewed laboratory measurements and
standardization to zero mean and unit variance. The result is reported in
Figure~\ref{fig:pca-variance}. No component dominates: the leading one accounts for
less than 15\% of the variance, ten of the fifteen components are needed to reach
80\% of it and twelve to reach 90\%. The continuous covariates are therefore only
weakly correlated with one another, and the single strong pairwise correlation in the
block is the one between baseline and pre-procedural creatinine ($r = 0.87$). The
leading component loads on exactly those two measurements, with coefficients $+0.61$
and $+0.60$ and with ejection fraction entering with the opposite sign ($-0.26$): it
orders patients by renal function rather than by any composite measure of severity.

Projecting the patients onto the leading components and colouring them by outcome
separates no endpoint, and the nonlinear embeddings agree.
Figure~\ref{fig:embeddings} applies t-SNE and UMAP to the dataset. The two methods recover different global
structure: t-SNE returns a single continuous cloud, whereas UMAP resolves the cohort
into roughly a dozen discrete islands. Neither produces a region in which events
concentrate. Every UMAP island contains patients from all three outcome groups, and in
the t-SNE cloud the event patients are spread across the whole manifold. The islands
are therefore a property of the covariate space, generated by recurring combinations
of the binary indicators, and they carry no outcome information.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{Non_linear_embeddings.png}
\caption{Two-dimensional embeddings of the first 30 principal components of the
standardized design, obtained with t-SNE (perplexity 30, left) and UMAP (15
neighbours, minimum distance 0.1, right). Both embeddings are unsupervised, the outcome being used
only for the colouring. The discrete islands in the UMAP panel each contain patients
of all three groups.}
\label{fig:embeddings}
\end{figure}

The same question can be put to the multivariate extremes: whether the ischemic events
concentrate in the few patients with the most unusual covariate profiles. Hotelling's
$T^2$ statistic was computed for every patient on the first ten principal components of
the full standardized design, and compared against the $\chi^2_{10}$ distribution that
it follows under normality. The result is reported in Figure~\ref{fig:outliers}. The
99.5\% quantile of that distribution, $T^2 = 25.2$, flags 162 of the 4,579 patients
(3.5\%) as extreme outliers, and the 99\% quantile, $T^2 = 23.2$, flags 206.

These patients do carry a higher event rate: cardiovascular death and stroke occur
roughly 3.4 and 3.5 times more often among them than among the rest of the cohort. The
group is nevertheless too small to account for the events. It contains 11.1\% of the
cardiovascular deaths and 11.4\% of the strokes, so close to nine ischemic events in ten
occur in patients whose covariate profile is not extreme. Screening on multivariate
extremes therefore isolates a higher-risk group but leaves most of the ischemic events
outside it. The right-hand panel of Figure~\ref{fig:outliers} adds a second observation:
a large share of the flagged patients lie away from the main body of the cohort in the
same direction, which is consistent with the extreme profiles being similar to one
another rather than scattered.

\paragraph{Pairwise interactions.}
The last exploratory question is whether the association between a covariate and an
outcome depends on the value of another covariate. An $L_1$-penalized logistic
regression was first fitted for bleeding at 335 days, the most frequent endpoint, on
the standardized design, with the penalty strength chosen by cross-validation.
Figure~\ref{fig:lasso-path} traces four representative coefficients as the penalty is
relaxed: \texttt{diabetes\_type\_ii\_die} and \texttt{prec\_hb} enter first, around
$C \approx 0.05$, and rise together; \texttt{p\_bleed\_action1\_rece} and
\texttt{diabetes\_type\_ii\_ins} enter simultaneously near $C \approx 1$, with opposite
signs, while \texttt{prec\_hb} dips at the same point. This joint, opposite-signed entry
suggests overlapping predictive information between the two pairs, motivating a more
formal interaction check.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{Lasso_Regularization_Path.png}
\caption{Regularization path of the $L_1$-penalized logistic regression for bleeding at
335 days: coefficient value as a function of $C$ (inverse penalty, log scale) for four
representative covariates. \texttt{diabetes\_type\_ii\_die} and \texttt{prec\_hb} are
the first to leave zero and they change direction of growth together when \texttt{p\_bleed\_action1\_rece} and
\texttt{diabetes\_type\_ii\_ins} activate together, with opposite sign, near

\label{fig:lasso-path}

The search was then repeated on a curated set of sixteen clinically interpretable variables, 
including the treatment indicator, expanded into all pairwise products. Seventeen terms survive 
the cross-validated L1 penalty, of which thirteen are interactions. The selected interactions and 
their coefficients are reported in Table~\ref{tab:selected_interactions}. None involves the treatment 
indicator. Greedy forward selection (Sequential Feature Selector, SFS) retains two of the thirteen interactions, 
achieving a cross-validated ROC-AUC of $0.553$ using interaction terms alone.

These terms are recorded but not pursued, because they answer a different question 
from the one this thesis asks: they describe associations among baseline covariates 
in predicting the outcome, rather than modifications of the treatment effect. 
An interaction between two baseline characteristics indicates that the risk surface is 
not additive; it does not by itself indicate that the benefit of abbreviated therapy varies across patients.
